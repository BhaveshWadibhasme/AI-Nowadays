AI Engineering

The core of building AI into real applications.

A model demo and an application are different jobs. The application has to know what it is allowed to see, what it is allowed to spend, whether it is still right, where it must stop, and which actions it may take. Those five jobs are the pillars below. Read together, they are what “AI in apps” means on a delivery. Read alone, each one is a way a pilot fails in front of a client.

Five pillars, one system

01 · Context

Give it context

Retrieval, memory, chunking, and the order of what the model sees. If this is wrong, the answer is wrong before generation starts.

 02 · Cost

Cut down the cost

Routing, prompt caching, smaller models, batching, and the latency you are willing to trade. A feature you cannot afford to run is not shipped.

 03 · Measure

Measure it

Offline and online evals, traces, and whether the answer sticks to its sources. A change you cannot score is a change you cannot defend.

 04 · Guardrails

Guardrails

Validation, prompt-injection defence, PII, policy filters, and a person on the step that sends, pays, or deletes.

 05 · Tools

Tools + agents

Function calling, MCP, loops, and the cases where a fixed workflow is the better design. Action comes last, and only inside a boundary.

The combination

AI in apps

A model call becomes an application when all five hold at once. Context without a cost limit, a measurement, a boundary, and a controlled action is a demo that happens to use an API.

In the room

When a client asks for “an AI feature”, start with the pillar that is missing. A model bake-off can wait. Five questions tell you whether you are scoping an application:

  • What may it read? Which sources count as true, how fresh they are, and who is allowed to see each one. That is context.
  • What may a successful answer cost? Which calls sit on the user path, and which can wait until tonight. That is cost.
  • How will we know it got worse? After the next prompt, model, or corpus change, which slice must not regress. That is measurement.
  • What must never happen? What it refuses, what it redacts, and what a person has to approve. That is guardrails.
  • What may it do? Which actions are reversible, which tools exist, and where a fixed workflow is enough. That is tools and agents.
Why it matters

If the room can answer all five, you can write a design. If it can answer one, you are looking at a demo. The primers are the review notes for each question: why it breaks in production, the techniques that hold, the pitfalls, and a checklist you can walk in a design review.

How the feed files in

The primers stay put. The news moves. Each pillar page ends with “Latest from the feed”, which reads digests.json and lists entries whose optional pillar field matches. Leave the field off and the issue stays in the digest only. Set it to one of context, cost, measure, guardrails, or tools-agents and the same issue appears under that primer.

File a digest when the news is a technique you will reuse with a client. The checklist on the primer is the stable note. The feed under it is the evidence that the note is still current.
