Event · 5 Nov · LangChain × TypeSafe AI

A night of talks on building with decision models

On November 5th, LangChain and TypeSafe AI are hosting an evening of talks on building with decision models. Sydney Runkle will share how decision models fit in an agent harness and a pattern for pairing them with LLMs: cheap by default, frontier on exception. Allie Laabs from TypeSafe AI will discuss why you shouldn't call it a decision model, and why you should think of it as a System One Model instead. Seán Roche from LangChain Academy will give a talk on Jev as a judge and what the future of agent evals looks like. RSVP is open.

Why it matters

“Cheap by default, frontier on exception” is a ready-made cost pattern for agents: keep routine decisions on a cheap model and reserve frontier LLMs for the exceptions. Worth tracking if your department's agent costs are climbing.

Explainer video · LangChain Academy

LangGraph in 1 minute, and when to use it

Ishaan Lal explains LangGraph in a one-minute video. Keeping an agent predictable over many steps, or across a multi-agent system, is hard: real agents have to decide what happens next, whether that's a tool call, a handoff to another agent, waiting on a person, or a retry. In plain code that turns into conditionals nobody can follow. LangGraph models it as a graph instead: nodes do the work (like calling a model), edges decide what's next, and a shared state holds everything produced, which every node can read and update. LangChain Academy offers a free course to learn more.

Why it matters

A crisp way to explain to stakeholders why multi-step or multi-agent workflows need explicit control flow, and a free course to point teammates to before they build one.

Live session · GTM Engineering Live · 28 Oct, 11 AM PT

Behind the scenes of LangChain's design agent

This Wednesday (Oct 28, 11 AM PT), LangChain is taking people behind the scenes of the design agent it uses every day. Built on Managed Deep Agents, it lives in Slack and works like an in-house creative studio. The team will demo it live, walk through how they built it, and share what they've learned using an agent for creative production across the company: going from a request in Slack to finished videos, images, writing and multi-channel campaign assets; designing an agent that plans and executes creative work instead of stopping at a first draft; and giving it brand context, memory, tools and real company data, plus how they handle long-running jobs, quality checks and human approvals. The floor will also be open for attendees' own use cases and questions.

In a reposted note, Shreya Talegaonkar (Brand Design @ LangChain) explains that “taste is the judgment that separates work that's correct from work that's good.” Brand tokens tell a model which colors and fonts to use, but not what a piece is for, so it fills every open decision with whatever it has seen most; when she gave it the note “No sharp edges,” the first version came back as a boxy line with hard edges. Working with Amal Irgashev, every note they give it comes with the reasoning behind it, and each one lands in a skill or an automated check. The agent has shipped 3,000+ assets since August. Amal Irgashev, Daniel Lambert, Jan Franco Glanc Gomez and Shreya are walking through how they taught it taste at the session.

Why it matters

A concrete production pattern for any internal agent: turn every piece of human feedback, with its reasoning, into a reusable skill or automated check, and build in quality checks and human approvals for long-running work.
