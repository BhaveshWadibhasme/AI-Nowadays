Funding · LangSmith

LangChain raises $300M at a $3B valuation and unveils the new LangSmith platform

LangChain has raised $300M at a $3B valuation to advance its mission to enable every company to own its intelligence. The Series C was led by existing investor CapitalG, with significant participation from new investors GIC and DFJ Growth and existing investors IVP and Sapphire Ventures.

Alongside the fundraise, LangChain introduced the new LangSmith platform, built for and powered by agents and covering the agent development lifecycle: build, run, improve and govern. More than 7,000 organizations use LangSmith in production, from startups like Clay, Harvey and Rippling to enterprises like NVIDIA, Toyota Motor Corporation and Workday. LangSmith Engine is free for all new Plus subscribers through the end of the year.

Why it matters

Funding is going into a framework-agnostic platform to run, improve and govern agents, which signals where LangChain sees demand: production operations and governance rather than prototypes.

Education · Managed Deep Agents

Managed Agents: a free Academy course and a 17 Nov workshop in San Francisco

LangChain Academy launched a course, Building and Deploying Managed Agents. Managed Agents (aka Managed Deep Agents) handles the runtime layer: you define the agent and its context, then deploy with one command. The course covers instructions and skills, running on the managed platform with its own sandbox and user credentials, authenticating users and connecting channels like Slack, and evaluating the agent.

On 17 November in San Francisco (5:30 PM), a hands-on workshop builds and deploys a data analysis agent with Managed Deep Agents on LangSmith: mda dev to test locally, mda deploy to ship, plus memory, connectors and scheduled runs.

Why it matters

Shipping an agent to real users usually takes weeks of surrounding infrastructure; a one-command managed runtime is aimed at cutting exactly that.

Ecosystem · Decision models

Day 0 support for Google's Gemini Decisions API

Google launched the Gemini Decisions API. In a repost, Sydney Runkle describes decision models as a primitive for making choices when the cost and latency of LLMs are too high: output is bounded to a fixed set of choices, which makes it more reliable to embed in software. gemini-decision-1 supports multimodal input such as PDFs, images and audio, and LangChain has day 0 support.

Why it matters

Bounded-output decision models are a cheaper, more predictable alternative to a full LLM call for routing and classification steps in an agent.

Case studies · LangSmith

Workday cut agent prototyping to under a day; Campfire traces its accounting agent

LangGraph and LangSmith took Workday's agent prototypes from 4 to 5 months down to under a day. With LangGraph, Agent Server and LangSmith, teams get agents running in staging with auth and security out of the box. LangChain spoke with Alexander Gatto, Senior Machine Learning Engineer at Workday.

In the LangSmith for Startups spotlight, Campfire, an AI-native ERP, uses LangSmith to trace every step of Ember, its accounting agent, to see how it reasons, which tools it calls and where it goes wrong.

Why it matters

Both are concrete production stories where tracing and a managed agent stack are credited for faster iteration and fewer customer-facing failures.

Community · Survey

State of Agent Engineering survey is open

LangChain is running its annual State of Agent Engineering survey to shape the 2027 report. It asks what kinds of agents teams are building, for which use cases, and how they evaluate agent behavior. It takes about 5 minutes, results will be published publicly, and respondents can win limited edition swag.

Why it matters

The published results should give a useful benchmark of how teams build and evaluate agents.

Talks · Evals and proactive agents

Interrupt NYC sessions, eval-driven development and a proactive agents talk

At Interrupt NYC, Harrison Chase held a fireside chat with Modal CEO and co-founder Erik Bernhardsson on sandboxes, inference and more, and Pedro Rodriguez, Director of ML at Airbnb, shared how his team built trust and safety agents on LangGraph.

Divya Santhanam of LangChain Academy walks through the three steps of eval-driven development, using evals to build agents that improve over time.

Sydney Runkle shared slides from her proactive agent engineering talk: the shift from "you give an agent tasks" to "an agent gives you tasks", which needs a step change in memory and UX.

Why it matters

Evals and memory/UX for proactive behaviour come up again and again as the next hard problems after getting an agent running.
