Systems thinkers as an organization of one

Futurist Sinéad Bovell sat down with Yossi Matias, VP of Google and GM of Google Research, on I've Got Questions. Their takeaway: the future doesn't belong to narrow specialists. It belongs to systems thinkers operating as an "organization of one." When AI handles the heavy lifting across code, synthesis, and technical execution, human value moves upstream: asking which problems are worth solving, connecting ethics, engineering, and product, judging trade-offs algorithms can't decide, and adapting as the toolkit changes.

Why it matters

The post's claim is that the valuable human work sits upstream of execution: choosing the problem, connecting disciplines, and judging trade-offs once AI can do the technical heavy lifting.

Research@ Mountain View: AI as an amplifier of inquiry

"There's truly never been a better time to be a scientist." Gemini 4 Argon compresses months of graph and TPU kernel work into hours

Google Research reshared Vahab Mirrokni, Google Fellow and VP. Graph Mining and Graph Learning used Gemini 4 Argon with Teamwork in Google Antigravity to compress months of low-level RDMA networking and custom TPU kernel development into hours. FarArray accelerated graph algorithms by up to 180x and used 22x less RAM. Custom TPU kernels for graph neural networks run 8–15x faster than native JAX, with large HBM savings. Mirrokni notes the loop: Graph Mining contributed to Gemini through deduping and data curation, and Gemini is now scaling Graph Mining. The full blog is "How Gemini 4 Argon Upleveled Agents on Antigravity: Trillion-Edge Graphs and Custom TPU Kernels."

Why it matters

The reported result is months of RDMA networking and custom TPU kernel work compressed into hours, with FarArray up to 180x faster and 22x less RAM, and GNN kernels 8–15x faster than native JAX. It is a case of an agent harness taking on low-level engineering, not only application code.
