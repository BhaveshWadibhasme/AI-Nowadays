Managed Deep Agents

Managed Deep Agents now work with files in Slack threads

Caspar Broekhuizen (Agents at LangChain) shared that Managed Deep Agents can read the files people share in Slack and send files back to the same thread. Someone drops a CSV in a thread and asks for a deck; the agent picks the file up in its sandbox (a computer with its own filesystem), builds the deck, and posts it back as a reply.

When a teammate asks for another slide later in the thread, the CSV and the first draft are still in the agent's filesystem, so it just updates the deck. The team keeps working in Slack and the agent works right there with them.

Why it matters

Agents that keep a persistent filesystem per thread can do multi-turn document work where people already collaborate, with no new tool to adopt.

Models · NVIDIA

Nemotron 3.5 Super VL brings vision to Deep Agents via ChatNVIDIA

NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Super VL brings vision, reasoning, and tool use to Deep Agents through ChatNVIDIA. An agent can interpret a customer's screenshot or a document image, reason about what it sees, and take action using its existing tools.

The open model has 120B total parameters and 12B active parameters, with NVIDIA NeMo tooling for post-training on your own data and workflows. It averaged an 87% pass rate on LangChain's Deep Agents behavioral suite, and on Harbor Unified-lite it used 55% less total task time than DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, with similar task success. It builds on LangChain's earlier work with Nemotron 3 Ultra.

Why it matters

A fast open-weight multimodal option for agents that need to read screenshots and documents, with a path to post-train on your own data.

Customer story · LangSmith

Opine gates every prompt and model change behind LangSmith evals

Opine is a customer intelligence layer for revenue teams: it captures deal context from calls, threads and rooms the CRM never sees, and feeds that intelligence to the agents teams already use. Docker, Tailscale, Gainsight, Orca Security, Socket.dev, and BigID use it; the post says Docker generates 100%+ more revenue per SE hour and Tailscale saves 100+ hours a week with Opine.

Opine traces every production LLM run through LangSmith and gates every prompt and model change behind LangSmith evals, so they can ship changes to their deal-context agents without guessing whether quality moved.

Why it matters

A clean example of eval-gated releases: no prompt or model change ships unless evals show quality held.
