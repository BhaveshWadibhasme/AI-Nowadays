Case study 01 · arXiv, 23 Oct 2026

AI Engineering · Context

Reimagining Enterprise RAG for Multi-Agent Systems: A Knowledge Layer That Organizes Before It Retrieves (arXiv, 23 Oct 2026)

100+production defects root-caused
35release-regression failures attributed
18services
241repositories

Problem

SDLC agents at a large enterprise needed the actual business rules of 18 services (recovered from 241 repositories into thousands of artifacts). The same terms (offer, eligibility, activation) mean different things in different services, so flat vector search confidently returned a near-identical rule from the wrong service, with no signal of error.

Approach

Instead of an expensive content-level knowledge graph, put the ontology at the artifact level. Each artifact keeps its provenance (producer, component, service, domain, type). A self-describing catalog, a sparse metadata registry, and a navigation hypergraph over artifacts resolve a query to a set of artifact IDs first; vector search is then constrained to that set, so the wrong-service rule is never a candidate at all.

Results

Built in under a week; new services join by convention. In production it serves SDLC agents plus developers, QA engineers and analysts. It supported root-cause analysis for more than 100 production defects (developer-verified) and attributed 35 release-regression failures to specific rules and services.

Lessons

Similarity is not correctness: the most dangerous RAG failure is the high-confidence wrong-context hit. Cheap structural filtering beats building a full knowledge graph whose cost exceeds the engagement and which goes stale every release.

Pitch for bp

Any internal RAG over documents from multiple assets, sites or business units has this exact failure (same procedure name, different site). Propose a metadata-first retrieval layer (asset, site, document type, revision) that scopes the candidate set before embeddings, and show a before/after on a set of “wrong-site” trick questions.

Case study 02 · arXiv, 13 Oct 2026

AI Engineering · Tools + agents

Plant Memory: A Verified Maintenance Ontology for Agent-Accessible Failure Knowledge from SAP PM Work Orders (arXiv, 13 Oct 2026)

82.1%top-1 root cause
53.6%raw SAP root-cause column
96.2%component accuracy
100%root-cause precision at 62% coverage

Problem

Maintenance work orders are the biggest written record of how equipment fails, but the text is abbreviated and misspelt, asset identity is buried in free text, and symptom, root cause and action fields are usually empty. Diagnostic knowledge leaves with senior technicians.

Approach

Technical language processing over SAP Plant Maintenance exports builds an ISO 14224-aligned ontology where every fact carries a confidence score, its source evidence and a verification state. Extraction is rule-first; an LLM acts as a second reader whose disagreements raise review priority rather than overwrite facts. Technicians verify through a review queue, answer targeted knowledge-gap questions, and approve ontology drift. The graph is served to people via a web app and to AI agents via an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server, with every answer citing work orders.

Results

On a synthetic 12-month export of 400 work orders: 96.2% component accuracy, 88.9% failure-mode accuracy, 100% root-cause precision at 62% coverage. On 28 diagnostic questions, ranked root causes were correct at top-1 in 82.1% of cases vs 53.6% for the raw SAP root-cause column. The LLM as an independent extractor was less accurate than the rules on every field.

Lessons

The data is synthetic and the authors say the numbers are optimistic; a real-plant evaluation is still needed. Keep the human verification loop and provenance; use the LLM as a checker, not the source of truth.

Pitch for bp

bp runs SAP-style maintenance at refineries and production sites. Pitch a small pilot on one equipment class (pumps or compressors): extract failure modes and root causes from historical work orders, have reliability engineers verify in a queue, and expose it to a copilot over MCP. The metric is top-1 root-cause accuracy vs the raw fields.

arXiv paperhttps://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19131

Case study 03 · Fogsphere, with NVIDIA Cosmos and Metropolis

AI Engineering · Guardrails

ADNOC's RASED: Safety Vision Agents in Production Across 65 Sites (Fogsphere, with NVIDIA Cosmos and Metropolis)

69live safety use cases
1,000+cameras
65sites
9ADNOC Group companies

Problem

Spotting unsafe acts and conditions across drilling rigs, upstream, gas processing, refining, petrochemicals and fuel stations, where the most important incidents are rare and hard (or unsafe) to collect footage for.

Approach

Fogsphere Vision Agent combines specialized computer vision with NVIDIA Cosmos vision-language reasoning and the Metropolis Video Search and Summarization blueprint. Inference runs at the edge on Dell infrastructure; only event intelligence goes to the central platform, and full video stays on site. MistIQ uses NVIDIA Cosmos to generate synthetic video for low-data and no-data scenarios, closing a deploy, find hard cases, synthesize, retrain, redeploy loop.

Results

In production in ADNOC's RASED (Real-time AI Safety Event Detection) programme: 69 live safety use cases, more than a thousand cameras, 65 sites, nine ADNOC Group companies. This is a vendor announcement; no accuracy or incident-reduction figures were published.

Lessons

Data sovereignty by design (video never leaves the site) is what makes multi-site rollout acceptable. Synthetic data fills rare-event gaps but needs careful evaluation against real footage. Treat vendor-reported scale as scale, not as proof of safety outcomes.

Pitch for bp

HSE is an easy executive sponsor. Propose a vision-language pilot on existing CCTV at one site for two or three well-defined hazards (PPE, exclusion zones, hot work), edge inference, events-only to the centre, and a measured false-positive rate before scaling.

Case study 04 · SoundHound AI, 27 Oct 2026

AI Engineering · Tools + agents

How Evergy Reduced Calls With AI (SoundHound AI customer story, 27 Oct 2026)

67%containment rate
4 million+calls handled a year
12.7 millioncalls deflected since 2020
1.7 millioncustomers, Kansas and Missouri

Problem

Evergy, an electric utility serving about 1.7 million customers in Kansas and Missouri, needed to scale customer service for billing, payment plans, outage inquiries and service starts and stops without losing the human touch.

Approach

Voice AI agents handle routine requests end to end, continuously improved jointly by Evergy and SoundHound, while human representatives take the complex or sensitive calls.

Results

67% containment rate, more than 4 million calls handled a year, and about 12.7 million calls deflected from the contact centre since 2020, at a lower cost per transaction. Related this month: ENGIE's Salesforce Agentforce deployment resolves 71% of cases autonomously at 83% accuracy.

Lessons

This is a six-year programme, not a quick win; containment rises through continuous tuning. Vendor-published figures. Keep a clean handoff to humans so callers don't repeat themselves.

Pitch for bp

Customer-facing and internal service desks (fuel cards, retail, B2B energy customers, IT and HR helpdesks) are a proven pattern. Pitch a narrow, transactional scope with containment rate, handle time and handoff quality as the KPIs from day one.

Case study 05 · BCG, 26 Oct 2026

AI Engineering · Measure

BCG: How to Create AI Value by Balancing Innovation and Risk (26 Oct 2026)

3xROI with innovation and governance
2xROI from innovation alone
1,330leaders surveyed
>30%higher ROI when decisions are codified

Problem

Leaders worry that governance slows AI down. BCG tested whether innovation and governance trade off.

Approach

Analysis from BCG's 2026 Applied AI Index survey of 1,330 leaders of global enterprises, comparing companies by levels of AI innovation and governance (“accountable innovators”).

Results

Scaling innovation alone gives 2x the ROI of low-innovation companies; scaling innovation alongside governance gives 3x. Organizations that record and codify AI decisions see more than 30% higher ROI than those that only record. High-accountability organizations are more than 2.5x as likely to have and deploy a kill switch, and 1.5-2x more likely to require AI tool, responsible-AI and role-specific training. This lines up with Gartner's IT Symposium finding that 71% expect to deploy agentic AI within 12 months but only 21% have agentic governance in place.

Lessons

Governance pays when it's operational (decision logs, kill switches, training), not a policy PDF. Survey-based correlation, not causation.

Pitch for bp

Bundle every agent proposal with a lightweight governance kit: a decision log, a named owner, a kill switch, and role-specific training. Frame it as the thing that makes the project scale, not the thing that slows it.

For the week

Consulting playbook

Organize knowledge before you retrieve it. The production wins this week scoped the agent's world first (metadata filters, verified facts with provenance, site-local video) and only then let models reason over it. When you pitch, lead with a narrow, measurable scope, a before/after metric (wrong-context hits, top-1 root cause, containment), and a governance kit that makes scaling safe. Also budget for scale from the start: ADNOC and SLB's drilling platform only showed its 30-40% cut in engineering effort per rig across 120+ rigs, not in a small pilot (company-reported).

Hands-on

Framework trend worth learning hands-on

Serving verified knowledge to agents over MCP with metadata-scoped retrieval. Build a small MCP server (the Python MCP SDK's FastMCP) that exposes a tiny maintenance knowledge base: facts with source, confidence and verified flag, and a search tool that filters by asset and site before vector search. Connect it to a LangGraph agent via langchain-mcp-adapters and compare answers against plain vector RAG on a handful of trick questions where the same term appears at two sites.
