Case study 01 · Databricks customer story, Nov 2026

Problem

Maintenance data was spread across ~60 source systems (47 SAP instances plus Maximo) in upstream, midstream, downstream and logistics. “Gulf of Mexico” was spelled 11 different ways. Engineers waited weeks on analysts to learn whether equipment might fail in the next 30 days.

Approach

Systems of record were moved onto Databricks under Unity Catalog. The silver layer maps SAP/Maximo terms to a shared model (equipment, work order, notification) with proper keys. The gold layer is modelled with domain experts. An AI data-readiness score covers 5 areas (quality, standardisation, metadata/lineage, usability, governance), and only data scoring 4-5 goes to agents. The Genie Agent is set up with engineering standards, MTBF definitions, table relationships and rules such as “always sum cost from the cost line table”. Users reach it through Teams and APIs.

Results

Questions that took weeks now take ~30 seconds. An MVP went to 400 users and is ready to expand to 9,000. On the same data with keys and column definitions, Genie answered 4x faster. Genie Code produces about 75% of a starting silver model.

Lessons

Data modelling and metadata are what make the agent work. A readiness gate keeps unprepared data away from agents. Business rules written into agent instructions prevent quiet calculation errors.

Pitch

This is your own company’s reference case. Offer to reuse the readiness score and Genie pattern on refinery data, for example coker/unit operations, lab results and maintenance for Shot Coke work. Ask for a 4-6 week MVP with a fixed question set and time-to-answer as the KPI.

Case study 02 · Databricks case study

PETRONAS PetroVisor: a supervisor plus domain agents over 1.8M engineering documents

Oil & Gas Multi-agent Agentic RAG LLM-as-judge MLflow
~90%of upstream data unstructured
1.8Mengineering documents
30-45 secdata discovery, from 3-5 days
>90%query accuracy, vendor and customer

Problem

About 90% of upstream data was unstructured and spread across OSDU, PI, SQL and 1.8M engineering documents. Engineers spent 3-5 days per data-discovery task.

Approach

Data was consolidated in Unity Catalog with a semantic layer. A supervisor orchestrator routes each query to Subsurface, Surface or Sensor agents. Retrieval uses vector search, hybrid search, RAG and Genie for structured data. Quality is checked with LLM-as-judge against ground-truth sets, and MLflow monitors it. The design is model-agnostic. Next on the roadmap is P&ID diagram reasoning.

Results

Data discovery went from 3-5 days to 30-45 seconds, with over 90% query accuracy (as reported by the vendor and customer).

Lessons

In their words, “architecture over LLM”. Domain agents with narrow scope are easier to evaluate than one general agent. You need ground-truth evals before you can claim accuracy.

Pitch

Propose a two-agent version: a document agent for SOPs and inspection reports and a sensor agent for PI historian data, with a supervisor routing between them. Start with a 50-100 question golden set for each domain.

Case study 03 · AWS for Industries, 12 Nov 2026

TGS Scout: from knowledge search to an agent that acts, with 99% citation grounding

Problem

Seismic processing runs around the clock in 7 offices across 15 time zones. Jobs failed after hours, HPC sat idle, and runbooks were scattered across Jira, a legacy tracker, Confluence and SharePoint, with the best fixes known only to a few senior experts.

Approach

Scout was built on AWS Quick with RAG and three rules. Every answer must cite a source, and Scout withholds an answer when the evidence is weak. Retrieval respects permissions: entitlements are attached to each chunk and filtered before the model sees them. It augments people rather than replacing them. Autonomy came in three stages: search, then a conversational assistant, then an agent. Scout now opens and updates Jira tickets and maintains documentation, and high-impact actions need human review. A user council (GeoCouncil, about 13 geoscientists representing 300+ colleagues) prioritised use cases and served as the pilot group.

Results

Across 35,000 responses over 8 months, 99% were grounded in citations and 70% were rated accurate by users. Time to find a solution fell from 2-3 hours to under 3 minutes (40-60x). Adoption was voluntary and reached 322 users, saving about 1,590 productivity days.

Lessons

70% accuracy is realistic for enterprise RAG (the post cites a typical 65-80% range), so plan for it rather than promising 95%. ACLs at chunk level are non-negotiable. A user council drives organic adoption.

Pitch

Pitch a “24/7 ops support copilot” for shift engineers over runbooks, incident history and MOC documents. Measure grounding rate, user-rated accuracy and time to resolve, and gate ticket actions behind approval.

Problem

Petrophysics planning and real-time log evaluation on a horizontal carbonate well were slow, manual, multi-tool work. At bpx energy, engineers read production data across thousands of wells by hand to find underperformers.

Approach

An in-house agentic assistant handled end-to-end petrophysics: planning packages, real-time log evaluation, automated subzone tagging that adjusts porosity and saturation cutoffs for lithology, and cement-bond and production-log preparation. Results were checked against an independent commercial-software interpretation. bpx’s Xpert, built with AWS, turns a natural-language question into a query, runs it and returns a plain-language answer with charts. Engineers keep every decision.

Results

Planning packages went from about half a day to about 5 minutes. Log-to-evaluation turnaround is under 4 minutes cached and 5-10 minutes from a cold start, and CBL/PLT preparation is done in the same shift. Xpert saves bpx about 15,000 engineer-hours a year. AWS separately cites 20-30% less unplanned downtime from predictive monitoring and an 8-12% output lift from production optimisation (vendor figures).

Lessons

Validating against an independent baseline is what earns trust from engineers. Keep the human as the decision-maker in operational workflows. Treat vendor aggregate figures as directional.

Pitch

Xpert is another bp-internal proof point. Reuse its “question, query, chart” pattern for refinery KPIs, and validate agent outputs against your existing ML or physics baselines, as you did with the ~20% Shot Coke uplift.

Case study 05 · Gartner, 16 Nov 2026 · arXiv: Trustworthy Execution of Industrial Agents

Governance gap: 95% had an agent incident and only 15% have circuit breakers

Governance Guardrails Industrial agents Research

55%say IT risk tools are outdated
70%believe agents operate within safe boundaries

0/29hazardous commits in the prototype

Problem

Agents are starting to change engineering systems (CAD, CAE, tickets, baselines), not just produce answers, while controls lag behind.

Approach

Gartner surveyed 298 US and UK I&O leaders and recommends runtime controls: classify agents as non-human identities, plus permission models, observability, audit, and circuit breakers for runaway loops. The arXiv framework sets out 8 control domains and a 4-plane architecture. It separates what a tool returned from the effect that actually happened, evidence coverage from engineering judgement, and historical results from whether they still apply.

Results

95% of organisations had at least one agent-related incident in the past 12 months. 57% call their governance weak, 55% say their IT risk tools are outdated, and only 15% have automatic circuit breakers, yet 70% believe their agents operate within safe boundaries. Gartner predicts 40% will suffer a business-critical disruption by 2028. The prototype recorded 0/29 hazardous commits, which the authors note is spec conformance and not a safety probability.

Lessons

“The call succeeded” does not mean “the change happened correctly”, so verify effects. Inventory every agent and give it an identity and least-privilege access.

Pitch

Add a one-page control plan to every agent proposal: agent identity and scopes, effect verification, circuit breaker and kill switch, audit log, and human sign-off points. Process safety teams in refining will recognise it as a layers-of-protection approach.

For the week

Consulting playbook

Sell the data foundation together with the agent. All four energy wins (bp Genie, PETRONAS, TGS, ADNOC) rest on a governed semantic layer, permission-aware retrieval and a golden evaluation set. Open each pitch with three items: a data-readiness score for the target data, one measurable question set with a baseline time-to-answer, and a staged autonomy path (answer, then cite, then act with approval). Point to bp’s own Genie (400 users, 9,000 planned) and bpx Xpert (15,000 hours a year) as internal proof that the pattern works here.

Hands-on

Framework trend

Semantic-layer agents (text-to-SQL) with LLM-as-judge evals. Model a small gold table with keys, column descriptions and metric definitions. Put a Databricks Genie space, or a LangGraph SQL agent, on top of it. Write 50 golden questions and score answers with MLflow 3 GenAI evaluation (LLM judges plus exact-match on SQL results). Then remove the metadata and measure the drop to reproduce bp’s 4x finding yourself.
