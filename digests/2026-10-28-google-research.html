Continual learning · 12 hours ago

AI Engineering · Context

A sleep phase so LLMs can keep learning

A new sleep phase in continual learning lets large language models take in new facts and improve their reasoning. It is meant to ease two standing limits: catastrophic forgetting, and knowledge that stays frozen at a cutoff.

Why it matters

Models that can absorb new facts after they are deployed, without wiping what they already know, are a direct response to a static knowledge cutoff. The sleep phase is that proposal, aimed at both memory and reasoning.

Energy access · 1 day ago

Ignite Energy Access: finding rooftop solar from space

More than 600 million people in sub-Saharan Africa live without electricity. Google's three-year collaboration with Ignite Energy Access maps rural paths and uses Gemini for Science plus drone imagery to detect tiny rooftop solar panels from space. The collaboration reports more than 160,000 tCO2e avoided and 3.7 million solar devices delivered since 2023.

Why it matters

The reported results since 2023 are more than 160,000 tCO2e avoided and 3.7 million solar devices delivered. The method combines mapped rural paths, Gemini for Science, and drone imagery that can pick out very small rooftop panels.

Highlighted

Trend watch

Google Research is pushing on three fronts. The first is models that keep learning after deployment, which is the sleep phase: a way for an LLM to take in new facts and reason better while easing catastrophic forgetting and a static knowledge cutoff. The second is agents that are rigorously evaluated in high-stakes domains such as health. The Personal Health Agent is that case, a multi-agent design with an evaluation framework, published in Nature and open-sourced. The third is cheap on-device and edge inference, from the Coral NPU milestones, plus benchmarks broad enough to cover many languages.
